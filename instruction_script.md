# Instruction script for the 4:6 pacer

The on-screen text in `pacer_circle_4-6_full_session_*.mp4` is the script. Read it word for word, so what participants hear matches what they read, and the captions double as the accessible version.

| Video time | On screen and spoken | Seconds available | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | In the next 10 minutes, you'll breathe along with this circle. | 10 | 11 |
| 0:10–0:22 | As it grows, breathe in gently through your nose. As it shrinks, breathe out slowly. | 12 | 15 |
| 0:22–0:34 | Keep each breath light and comfortable. There's no need to breathe deeply. | 12 | 12 |
| 0:34–0:44 | Sit upright and keep your eyes on the circle. | 10 | 9 |

At a calm pace of about 2 words per second, every card leaves 3 to 5 seconds of quiet. Start speaking about half a second after each card fades in.

## Delivery

Use an even, conversational voice, the way you'd explain a task, not a meditation voice. A slowed, hushed delivery works as a relaxation cue on its own, and it's hard to match in the Finding Focus arm.

Record in a quiet room, mono, and normalize to about −16 LUFS. Put no music or room tone under the voice.

## What the wording avoids, and why

"Deeply" appears only in the negative. Szulczewski (2019) found that an instruction to avoid deep breathing reduced the drop in end-tidal CO₂ and hyperventilation symptoms at 0.1 Hz.

There's no "notice," "sensations," "present moment," "let thoughts pass," or "relax." That keeps meditation and relaxation-suggestion content out, so the manipulation stays respiratory.

Exhalation route is left open ("breathe out slowly"). If you want to match the Laborde protocol exactly, replace the card at 0:10 with:

> As it grows, breathe in gently through your nose. As it shrinks, breathe out slowly through slightly pursed lips.

That's 19 words in 12 seconds, which is tight. If it feels rushed, drop "gently." Pick one version and use it for everyone.

## Optional rationale line (only if matched in the Finding Focus arm)

If you decide to give an expectancy-raising rationale, it has to be equally positive and equally specific for both conditions, and you should measure credibility and expectancy afterward. A neutral version for this arm:

> This exercise uses a slow, steady breathing rhythm that many people find helpful when they want to feel more settled.

Put it on the Qualtrics page before the Start button, not in the video, so you can drop it without re-rendering.
